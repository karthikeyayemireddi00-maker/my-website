Krishna

Lord Krishna

Krishna guided the Pandavas and became Arjuna's charioteer during the Kurukshetra war. He delivered the Bhagavad Gita and taught duty, wisdom, and righteousness.

Parashurama

Lord Parashurama

Parashurama was the sixth avatar of Vishnu and master of warfare. He trained Bhishma, Dronacharya and Karna.

Bhishma

Bhishma Pitamah

Bhishma sacrificed his happiness and vowed celibacy to serve Hastinapura loyally during war.

Dronacharya

Dronacharya

Dronacharya trained the Pandavas and Kauravas and was one of the greatest masters of warfare.

Kripacharya

Kripacharya

Kripacharya trained the Pandavas and Kauravas and was one of the greatest masters of warfare.

Vyasa

Sage Vyasa

Vyasa authored the Mahabharata and guided generations through wisdom and foresight.

Yudhishthira

Yudhishthira

Known for truth and righteousness, Yudhishthira became king after the Pandavas’ victory.

Arjuna

Arjuna

Arjuna was the greatest archer of his era and Krishna’s trusted companion in battle.

Duryodhana

Duryodhana

Duryodhana led the Kauravas and refused to give the Pandavas their rightful kingdom.

Karna

Karna

Karna was a loyal friend of Duryodhana and one of the strongest warriors in Mahabharata. Though born to Kunti, he fought against the Pandavas without knowing they were his brothers.

Bhima

Bhima

Bhima was known for immense strength and courage. He defeated Duryodhana and fulfilled his vows during the Kurukshetra war.

Nakula

Nakula

Nakula was admired for his beauty, sword fighting skills and knowledge of horses. He remained loyal to his brothers throughout exile and war.

Sahadeva

Sahadeva

Sahadeva was wise and skilled in astrology. He helped the Pandavas with planning and strategy during war.

Shakuni

Shakuni

Shakuni manipulated Duryodhana and played a major role in creating conflict between the Pandavas and Kauravas.

Abhimanyu

Abhimanyu

Abhimanyu was a brave young warrior who entered the Chakravyuha and fought fearlessly before being unfairly surrounded.

Ghatotkacha

Ghatotkacha

The son of Bhima and Hidimba, Ghatotkacha used magical powers in war and sacrificed himself to save Arjuna.

Ashwatthama

Ashwatthama

Ashwatthama, son of Dronacharya, became consumed by revenge after the war and was cursed to wander endlessly.

Draupadi

Draupadi

Born from sacred fire, Draupadi became the wife of the Pandavas and symbolized strength, dignity and justice.

Kunti

Kunti

Kunti raised the Pandavas through hardship and made sacrifices that deeply influenced the story of Mahabharata.

Gandhari

Gandhari

Gandhari blindfolded herself to share her husband's blindness and mourned deeply after losing her sons.
