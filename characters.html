Characters

Krishna

Lord Krishna

Krishna guided the Pandavas and became Arjuna's charioteer during the Kurukshetra war. He delivered the Bhagavad Gita and taught duty, wisdom and righteousness.

Parashurama

Lord Parashurama

Parashurama was the sixth avatar of Vishnu and master of warfare. He trained Bhishma, Dronacharya and Karna.

Bhishma

Bhishma Pitamah

Bhishma sacrificed his happiness, vowed celibacy and loyally served Hastinapura during the Kurukshetra war.

Dronacharya

Dronacharya

Dronacharya was teacher of both Pandavas and Kauravas and one of the greatest masters of warfare.

Vyasa

Sage Vyasa

Vyasa authored the Mahabharata and guided many during difficult times with wisdom and foresight.

Yudhishthira

Yudhishthira

Known for truth and righteousness, Yudhishthira became king after the Pandavas' victory.

Arjuna

Arjuna

Arjuna was the greatest archer of his era and Krishna's companion during the Kurukshetra war.

Duryodhana

Duryodhana

Duryodhana led the Kauravas and refused to give the Pandavas their rightful kingdom.

Karna

Karna

Karna was a loyal friend of Duryodhana and one of the strongest warriors in Mahabharata.

Bhima

Bhima

Bhima was famous for immense strength and fulfilled his vows during the Kurukshetra war.

Nakula

Nakula

Nakula was known for beauty, sword fighting skills and expertise in horse care. He remained loyal to his brothers throughout exile and war.

Sahadeva

Sahadeva

Sahadeva was wise, intelligent and skilled in astrology. He helped the Pandavas with strategy and fought bravely in war.

Shakuni

Shakuni

Shakuni was the mastermind behind many conflicts. His manipulation and dice game led to the exile of the Pandavas.

Abhimanyu

Abhimanyu

Abhimanyu was a fearless young warrior who entered the Chakravyuha and fought bravely before being unfairly surrounded.

Ghatotkacha

Ghatotkacha

The son of Bhima and Hidimba, Ghatotkacha used magical powers in battle and sacrificed himself to protect Arjuna.

Ashwatthama

Ashwatthama

Ashwatthama, son of Dronacharya, became consumed by revenge after the war and was cursed to wander endlessly.

Draupadi

Draupadi

Born from sacred fire, Draupadi became wife of the Pandavas and symbolized dignity, strength and justice.

Kunti

Kunti

Kunti raised the Pandavas through hardship and made sacrifices that deeply shaped the Mahabharata.

Gandhari

Gandhari

Gandhari blindfolded herself to share her husband's blindness and mourned deeply after losing her sons.

Kripacharya

Kripacharya

Kripacharya was a wise teacher and warrior who survived the Kurukshetra war and continued guiding future generations.
